Support

Levance is a film camera for your iPhone. Load a roll, shoot it frame by frame, and see your pictures once they're developed in the darkroom.

For Levance 1.0 on iPhone and iPad.

Questions

Where are my pictures?

In the darkroom. Tap Darkroom on the camera, and your rolls are filed in binders on the shelf, 25 rolls to a binder. Tap a binder to open it on its contact sheet, then tap a frame to see it large.

How do I save a frame to Photos?

Open the frame in the darkroom and tap save. You can keep the film borders or leave them off. Saved frames go into an album called Levance in Photos.

Can I take a frame again, or bring in a photo from my library?

No, and that's on purpose. Every press of the shutter uses a frame, as on film, and every frame is taken through the camera. You can discard frames you don't want in the darkroom; their places on the film stay blank.

How do I finish a roll?

Tap the roll at the bottom left of the camera, then Rewind and Develop. The roll goes back into its cassette and onto the shelf, and you can load the next film.

Why are my pictures orange, or blue?

Film can't change its white balance. Daylight film comes out warm under lamps, and lamplight film comes out blue in daylight. The camera tells you when the light doesn't suit the film you've loaded.

What's the difference between Spectral and Classic?

Spectral simulates the film each stock is modelled on, from that film's published data. Classic is Levance's own hand-tuned look. New frames come out Spectral; you can switch any colour frame between the two in the darkroom.

How do I turn on location or the date back?

Tap the gear on the camera to open Settings. Location notes where each frame is taken; the date back prints the date in the corner of 35mm frames.

Will my rolls move to a new phone?

Your rolls and frames are kept inside the app and come with your phone's backup, so restoring a backup or moving to a new iPhone brings them along. Deleting the app deletes them from the phone; frames you've saved to Photos stay there.
